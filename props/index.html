The real story of this ballot: one tax, two kill switches

Three of the fourteen measures are really one fight. Prop 40 is a one-time 5% tax on California billionaires. Props 41 and 42 never mention it, but each could void it in court if it draws more yes votes than Prop 40 does. One committee, "Building a Better California," backed by Sergey Brin and L. John Doerr III, is funding all three sides of that play at once.

Prop 40
The target

A one-time 5% tax on net worth above $1 billion, sponsored by the SEIU-UHW health care workers union to fund health care. About 200 to 214 billionaires would owe it.

Prop 41
Kill switch one

Sold as tax audits and transparency. Its spending-limit clause targets exactly the exemption Prop 40's revenue relies on, without ever naming it.

Prop 42
Kill switch two

Sold as protecting retirement savings. It bans taxes like Prop 40's outright, including retroactive ones, which is precisely how Prop 40's January 1, 2026 residency cutoff works.

Why your votes interact: under California's conflicting-measures rule, if Prop 41 or Prop 42 passes with more yes votes than Prop 40, courts could void Prop 40 even if Prop 40 itself passes. On September polling, Prop 42 was outpolling Prop 40, which is the scenario that kills the tax. Voting on these three is one decision, not three.

What is still unsettled

  • Campaign totals will change. The money figures come from the FPPC top-contributor list as of October 5, 2026 and Ballotpedia aggregates. Late-October filings will differ, sometimes by a lot.
  • Prop 38's biggest donor figure is reported, not filing-verified. The claim that Gary Michelson's entities gave the Yes campaign $50 million or more comes from California Today's reporting and was not independently verified against filings; the $31.2 million figure is the documented share of the $48.2 million yes total.
  • Prop 40's revenue is genuinely unknown. SEIU-UHW claims $100 billion; academic analysts call that optimistic. The real number depends on valuations of illiquid assets, how many billionaires are ruled residents, and litigation.
  • Whether 41 or 42 would actually void 40 is a court question. The voter guide warns it could happen; nothing is settled until judges rule after the election.
  • Polls will move. PPIC's October survey was expected in late October and could shift the close races (Props 39, 40, 42, 45).
